## What counts as عرنجية

Arabic on the surface and foreign underneath: Arabic words and correct case
endings, but the sentence frame, the word's sense, the pairing of words or
the image follows English or French (ص 8، 33). Loanwords and the famous
calques (لعب دورًا، تمّ إرسال، تغذية راجعة) are the visible layer. Most of
the problem lies in construction and word sense, so a text free of the
famous calques is not clean for that reason (ص 8، 45). Look at polished
prose as closely as sloppy prose. Teachers, stylists and authors of
usage guides write it too, and original (untranslated) Arabic carries the
same patterns as translations (ص 8، 24، 28).

## Judging a borderline case

1. **Grammatical is not the test.** A sentence can parse perfectly and
   still be foreign. Grammar (نحو) means following how Arabs build speech,
   not only getting the endings right. Absurd-calque test: «حدث الأمر
   خارجًا من الأزرق» is grammatical, and every reader rejects it. If a
   familiar phrase differs from that only in being familiar, familiarity
   is all that defends it. ص 36–37، 47
2. **Familiarity is not evidence.** Readers stop noticing a calque they
   meet constantly, so "it sounds natural" from a reader raised on
   translated prose doesn't clear it (ص 37، 40، 78). None of these clears
   a phrase either: an academy ruling (ص 50), a derivation found after the
   fact (ص 39، 70), "no grammarian forbids it" (ص 48), a famous writer who
   used it (ص 79), or calling it "modern standard Arabic" (ص 23).
3. **Provenance, not novelty.** New expressions that come from an Arabic
   faculty are sound, and resembling a European phrase is not enough to
   flag something (ص 16، 38). Coinages for genuinely new concepts, such as
   technical terms, are fine. The fault is a foreign word or sense for a
   meaning Arabic already expressed. Ask whether Arabic had a way to say
   this before the translation era. If it did, offer that. ص 40، 72
4. **Find the native form it displaced.** A calque rarely fills a gap. It
   replaces a native construction (كأنّه يكتشف ← يبدو كما لو كان يكتشف) and
   leaves it dead in writing. Offer that native form. If everyday speech
   still uses it (ثوب صوف، نمت ثلاث ساعات، قسا قلبه، أضحكني), the rewrite
   is safe. ص 41، 44، 59، 63
5. **Watch for helper words that stand in for a derived form.** English has
   no تصغير, intensive, تفاعل, causative stem, inflected comparative,
   predicate participle or نعت سببي. عرنجية replaces each of them with a
   helper word: جعل، أكثر، بعضهم بعضًا، كثيرًا، صغير، كان + صفة. ص 67
6. **Weigh density.** Translation also thins Arabic without producing a
   single wrong sentence: a native form dies (الإماتة), only the option that
   matches English survives (الاستحياء), or the matching option crowds out
   the others (التغليب). A single 🟡 item is a suggestion. A text where
   nearly every choice mirrors English (بعض، سوف، فقط، كان + صفة, all in a row)
   deserves a note on the pattern. Calques also cluster: one sentence by a
   well-known writer often holds two or three, and a calque from one domain
   (sex, family, unity) is a reason to check its neighbors. ص 56، 72، 74، 80
7. **Long and vague against short and native.** A heavy multi-word
   expression where a short native word exists (العمل الجنسي for الجماع) is
   a sign of a calque. ص 73
8. **Check the sense against the root.** When a native word is used in a
   sense its own meaning doesn't support (سلبي for bad, حلم for hope), look
   for the English word whose range it copies. Calqued senses also lead
   readers to misread older texts (صفة سلبية in الرازي is a negated
   attribute, not a bad one). ص 69، 71
9. **Severity.** Give 🔴 when the book treats the pattern as plainly
   foreign and the native wording is just as clear. Give 🟡 when the book
   is less firm, the native form is a preference rather than a correction,
   or the alternative is heavier in the context. Never give 🔴 on a guess.

## Rewriting

1. **Rebuild from meaning; don't patch words.** Translated prose stays
   captive to the source order. Rebuild the sentence: verb first where
   natural, Arabic linking, and the verb that the noun phrase was standing
   in for. ص 27
2. **Native, not archaic.** Use plain current words, not rare dictionary
   words. The early Arabs mocked people who overused them. A rewrite can
   sound unfamiliar to a modern reader without being difficult or wrong,
   but it should never be obscure. ص 43
3. **Revive the specific word.** A calqued sense usually displaced several
   native words (محاسن، مساوئ، معايب، مثالب، آفات). Pick the one that fits
   the context instead of one generic substitute. ص 70
4. **Reach for constructions English lacks:** النعت السببي (قومٌ كثيرةٌ
   أفضالُهم), الصفة المشبهة + إضافة (رجلٌ حادُّ الأنف), تفاعل (تعاونوا),
   causative stems (أضحكه), أفعل + مضاف إليه (أحرص الناس), صيغ المبالغة
   (كذوب), the predicate participle (أنا سائلك), the bare ظرف for a duration
   (أقام يومين), tanween for the indefinite (سقاه ماءً), إنما and ما … إلا
   for restriction, and emphasis built into the sentence (إنّ، لَـ، ـنّ).
   ص 55–67
5. **Test against a classical sentence that says the same thing.** When the
   entry cites a Qur'anic, hadith or classical wording, model the rewrite
   on it. ص 79
6. **Explain old words with native synonyms.** When glossing a classical
   word, don't explain it with the calque (الإنعاظ ← الانتصاب). ص 76
7. **Reference writers.** Writers born before about 1300 AH (c. 1883) are
   largely free of the style. A construction attested in their prose leans
   native. One attested only after them leans calque. ص 34
8. **If the user asks how to improve their own ear:** taste follows what
   you read most. Build it on pre-translation texts (Qur'an, hadith,
   classical prose) first, then read modern stylists. ص 29، 40، 80

## Do not flag

These look suspicious to a modern ear, or overlap with a cue in the
references, but are sound Arabic.

| Expression | Why it's sound | ص |
|---|---|---|
| زوجه / زوجك for a wife (and زوجة too) | Qur'anic (أمسك عليك زوجك). Both are sound, and زوج is the more eloquent | 16، 25 |
| النعت السببي: ثمراتٍ مختلفًا ألوانُها، القرية الظالمِ أهلُها، خالدٌ أذكياءُ عيالُه | Qur'an, hadith, 19th-century prose and living speech. Never call it stilted | 44، 56–57 |
| الصفة المشبهة مضافة إلى فاعلها: حادّ الأنف، الأحمر الشعر، عظيم المنظر | Native. It became rare only because English lacks it | 57 |
| صيغ المبالغة and اسم الفاعل as predicate: حلّاف، كذوب، أنا سائلك، ما أنا بتابع | Native and alive in speech. The "forced" feel comes from English habits | 66 |
| الأفعل agreeing with its noun to mark the utmost degree: الأسماء الحسنى، العروة الوثقى | Classical use. SYN-013 targets comparison within a group | 49 |
| كان + صفة for a lasting trait: كان كريمًا | MOR-003 targets be + adjective standing in for an action or change (the limit is inferred) | 63 |
| سلبي / إيجابي in logic and theology: صفة سلبية، قضية موجبة | The original sense: negation and affirmation | 71 |
| صديق حميم / صاحب حميم | The native sense of حميم | 75 |
| أخذ / يؤخذ من for inferring: يؤخذ من الآية كذا | Close to native usage. USE-007 targets أخذ for tolerating | 78 |
| حرفيًّا for word for word: نقله حرفيًّا | The literal sense. VOC-010 targets the intensifier | 7 |
| Technical coinages for new concepts: وحدة قياس، الجوهر والعرض | New terms for new ideas are sound | 72 |
| على الرغم من / رغم | The book faults بالرغم من (SYN-012) but hasn't ruled on these in the pages read | 31، 53 |
