## p8 · CRITERION · single calques are the small part
- note: completes the p7 thread. Isolated coinages (صنعت يومي and the like) grate on any native ear, but avoiding them while keeping the rest is like giving up sugar while eating fat: the bulk of the foreignness lies elsewhere, in structure. The author defines العرنجية as text that is Arabic in its surface and foreign in its inner build. For phase 2: a text free of loanwords and obvious idiom calques is not thereby clean; the skill should look past vocabulary.

## p8 · CRITERION · where it hides: skilled writers
- note: the author targets not untrained writers but the elite (writers, Arabic teachers, even authors of books correcting usage), because readers trust their prose and the foreignness there is subtler. For phase 2: apply the check to polished, otherwise correct prose, not only to sloppy text.

## p16 · CRITERION · what counts as الفصيح
- note: sound Arabic is what agrees with Arab speech in its rules, styles and usages; where Arab usage differs, the Quran's Arabic settles it
- note: later writers coined new terms, idioms and proverbs and still wrote sound Arabic, because the new forms came from an Arabic faculty; novelty alone is not the test, foreign provenance is

## p23 · CRITERION · "الفصحى المعاصرة" is not a looser standard
- resolves: carry from p22 (التدوين بعربية لا عامية ولا فصيحة)
- note: the author treats so-called contemporary fusha as a hybrid: correct in case endings and vocabulary, foreign in build. He rates it more harmful than dialect, because a dialect reader knows the gap and a fusha reader doesn't.
- note: he grants that a classical reader would struggle with modern prose, but denies this is the language growing; it's drift toward foreign usage. For phase 2: "it's modern standard Arabic" is not a defense of a calque; the test stays provenance (see p16 what counts as الفصيح).

## p27 · CRITERION · the mechanism: translation bound to the source text
- resolves: carry from p26 (al-Shayyal on Raphael Zakhour)
- note: the early translators' rule was full fidelity to the original: swap each foreign word for an Arabic one in the same slot. The result reads as translated even when every word is Arabic. For phase 2: a sentence whose parts sit in the source language's order and composition is the core sign, independent of vocabulary.

## p27 · METHOD · rearrange the sentence, don't keep the source order
- note: al-Shayyal credits Rifa'a with letting himself move the parts of a sentence, which got closer to acceptable Arabic; a translator quoted on the same page admits his phrasing drifted from Arabic because he stayed "captive" to the original's structure, composition and order. For REM: when rewriting, rebuild the sentence around Arabic order (verb first where natural, Arabic linking) instead of patching words.

## p28 · CRITERION · foreignness spreads beyond translation
- note: the first translators had a sound Arabic ear, so their foreignness came only from clinging to the source. The next generations learned their Arabic from those translated textbooks and then wrote original Arabic in the same French styles, usages and metaphors. For phase 2: don't assume a text is clean because it wasn't translated; original modern Arabic prose carries the same patterns.
- note: the author contrasts Rifa'a's own التلخيص (sound, eloquent) with his translations: the same writer is fluent when not tied to a source text

## p29 · METHOD · taste follows what you read most
- note: an ear is shaped by what it is fed constantly, not occasionally; reading two or three eloquent books after a lifetime of weak prose won't fix it, because taste is judged by the bulk. Seed for REM (ch.4 تعلم اللسان).

## p29 · CRITERION · deliberate foreignness in the Van Dyck Bible
- note: the author presents the 1281 AH Protestant Bible translation as a major source of the style. Its makers dropped al-Yaziji for making it too eloquent, and al-Karmali says only its letters and words are Arabic, not its phrasing. Background for phase 2, but it confirms that Arabic vocabulary with foreign phrasing is the target.
